# Independent Review of the Genre Taxonomy

## Verdict

The research is strong, but the six-archetype cut mixes three different analytical levels:

1. **What the player repeatedly manipulates** — people, flows, networks, environment, institutions.
2. **How a campaign is paced** — sandbox, authored escalation, bounded runs.
3. **How variance is delivered** — generated maps, applicants, incidents, drafts, markets.

Those should not be peer categories. “Roguelite drafted builder” is a campaign/variance format, not a fundamental simulation loop, and it should be removed entirely given the owner's preference. “Authored pressure-cooker” is primarily a pacing structure layered over resource allocation and governance. Meanwhile “macro organizer” and “physics/logistics engineering” each combine multiple distinct kinds of play.

## Recommended re-slice: classify by the player's recurring verb

### 1. Production and logistics optimizer

**References:** Factorio, Anno, transport and factory games.

**What play feels like:** Place producers and consumers, connect them, watch material flow, find starvation or backups, change ratios and routes, then scale the machine.

**Recurring decision:** “Where is throughput being lost, and is the correct response more capacity, a shorter route, storage, prioritization, or a different production chain?”

**Spatial logic:** Input/output distance, transport capacity, network topology, resource sites, waste handling.

**Fun source:** Visible causality, mastery, elegant automation, and scaling spectacle.

### 2. Network and land-use planner

**References:** SimCity, Cities: Skylines.

**What play feels like:** Draw networks and zones, observe decentralized growth, read congestion/coverage/desirability overlays, then reshape the city without breaking it.

**Recurring decision:** “Which network or land-use change will relieve this local problem without exporting it elsewhere?”

**Spatial logic:** Access, traffic hierarchy, service reach, land value, terrain, pollution, adjacency externalities.

**Fun source:** Watching a self-organizing place emerge from the framework the player drew.

This is not simply “macro.” It is specifically **indirect growth through networks and fields**.

### 3. Environmental systems engineer

**References:** Oxygen Not Included, Stationeers, parts of Timberborn.

**What play feels like:** Read physical overlays, capture or convert matter and energy, contain byproducts, and rebuild when heat/pressure/waste migrates through the system.

**Recurring decision:** “How do I transform this physical problem without creating a worse one?”

**Spatial logic:** Local material fields, gravity, temperature, pressure, contamination, insulation, redundancy.

**Fun source:** Mastering lawful physical causality and constructing a machine that genuinely works.

Factorio should not be grouped here: logistics networks and environmental process simulation produce different decisions.

### 4. Institution and service operator

**References:** Prison Architect, Two Point Hospital, Startopia, Theme Hospital.

**What play feels like:** Design a facility, admit a population, schedule and staff services, observe queues and behaviors, then intervene when the institution fails under load.

**Recurring decision:** “How should this facility process these people safely, quickly, and profitably?”

**Spatial logic:** Entrances, circulation, queues, staff travel, access control, incompatible populations, service catchments.

**Fun source:** Watching architecture and policy digest unpredictable human flow.

“Throughput-of-chaos” describes this evocatively, but **institution/service operations** is more exact. Chaos is optional; population flow under rules is the defining structure. Tropico does not belong here.

### 5. Agent-story colony

**References:** RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress, early/small-scale Space Haven.

**What play feels like:** Set priorities and plans, watch autonomous individuals execute them, inspect needs and relationships, then triage threats involving people the player knows.

**Recurring decision:** “Who can do this, what must be sacrificed, and whose personal situation makes the obvious plan dangerous?”

**Spatial logic:** Work travel, defense, personal needs, social compatibility, safe zones, local resources.

**Fun source:** Attachment, improvisation, and memorable emergent stories.

### 6. Society and political-economy manager

**References:** Songs of Syx, Tropico, later-scale Dwarf Fortress, parts of Victoria-style management.

**What play feels like:** Allocate population groups, labor, imports, institutions, rights, and public goods; manage constituencies whose interests are structurally incompatible; convert settlement scale into political and economic power.

**Recurring decision:** “Which group or sector benefits from this policy and expansion, who pays, and can the resulting coalition remain stable?”

**Spatial logic:** Districts, workforce geography, class/species needs, distribution, trade access, prestige and exclusion.

**Fun source:** Statecraft, scale transition, competing constituencies, and seeing a settlement become a civilization.

Songs of Syx should not share a bucket with Cities: Skylines. One is primarily labor/population/state organization; the other is network-mediated urban growth.

### 7. Crisis governance

**References:** Frostpunk, parts of Surviving Mars scenarios.

**What play feels like:** Forecast an escalating threat, allocate scarce labor and resources, enact consequential policies, survive a stress test, and live with the social cost.

**Recurring decision:** “What must I sacrifice now to survive the next threshold, and what kind of society will that make?”

**Spatial logic:** Scarce safe radius, resource access, emergency travel, triage and resilience.

**Fun source:** Tension, irreversible tradeoffs, and judgment.

This is partly a pacing architecture, but it produces a distinct enough recurring decision to retain—with the caveat that it often overlays another simulation type.

## Dimensions that belong outside the archetype list

Every proposed game identity should also be plotted independently on these axes:

- Persistent station ↔ disposable/bounded run.
- Individual people ↔ aggregate populations.
- Direct intervention ↔ indirect policy/design.
- Stable optimization ↔ state-contingent adaptation.
- Continuous sandbox ↔ scheduled pressure cycles.
- Authored world ↔ generated world.
- Physical-world interaction ↔ panel-mediated interaction.
- Survival ↔ commerce ↔ social legitimacy as the primary score.

Variance sources should be catalogued separately: geography, physical fields, population composition, market demand, stakeholder agendas, traffic/intake, incidents, and player-created path dependence. A game can use any of these without becoming a different archetype.

## Where the current station game actually sits

“Throughput-of-chaos with the chaos removed” is memorable, but slightly too confident. The game currently sits primarily in **institution/service operations crossed with a lightweight tycoon**:

- Its most complete chain is berth → visitor → service rooms → credits.
- Its most valuable spatial foundations are circulation, queues, jobs, logistics, and room service.
- Visitors are not yet differentiated or adversarial enough to create Prison Architect-style behavioral pressure.
- The economy is not yet deep enough to create Anno-style production strategy.
- People are not distinctive enough for agent-story play.
- Environmental systems exist but are not consequential enough for ONI-style engineering.
- The system map, factions, residents, and command layer hint at political economy, but are presently mostly latent.

So the foundation naturally supports **several nearby pivots**, not one predetermined identity:

1. Deepen service operations into a watchable station/facility game.
2. Deepen commerce and logistics into a station tycoon.
3. Activate environment and failures into a systems-engineering game.
4. Activate people into an agent-story colony.
5. Grow populations/factions into a political-economy station.
6. Combine selected layers—but only after naming which recurring verb remains primary.

## Corrections to the path slate

- Remove the roguelite path and all blueprint/license drafting language. World variance should arise from simulated economies, populations, geography, stakeholders, and incidents—not perk selection.
- Do not describe finite physical ship traffic as a “draft.” Berth scheduling and admission are ordinary operations mechanics when ships exist in the world; they become card-like only when abstracted into offers detached from space and time.
- Split “Wired Throughput Tycoon” into **service operations** and **commerce/logistics tycoon** variants; they differ in minute-to-minute diagnosis.
- Add **political-economy station** as a first-class path. It best captures Songs of Syx/Tropico lessons and the CEO's transition from operator to regional institution.
- Treat game feel/watchability as necessary infrastructure across every path, not a separate optional scoring bonus.
- Treat stakeholder mandates as a political system only if stakeholders have persistent interests and power. Random missions from talking portraits are still event cards with faces.

## Bottom line

The taxonomy should answer one question before discussing variance or campaign structure:

> **What object does the player repeatedly inspect, manipulate, and improve?**

Flows, networks, environment, institutions, individuals, society, and crisis allocation produce genuinely different play. Once that primary loop is chosen, world variance and pacing can be designed to keep it from becoming stale without importing a roguelike structure.
