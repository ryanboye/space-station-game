# Strategic And Emergency Services Portfolio

Status: partial foundations; no coherent operating economy

## Player Promise

Operate a secure rescue, customs, diplomatic or military installation whose value is
readiness. The player reserves expensive capacity for uncertain events and balances
public commerce against control, resilience and response time.

This portfolio should not turn the game into constant combat. Most play is preparation,
traffic policy, secure movement and visible response to bounded incidents.

## Existing Foundations

- Security room, Terminal, cameras, Access Gates and Brig;
- Security Guards and officers;
- theft, trespass, fighting, local control and reputation;
- Clinic, Med Bed, Doctors and Nurses;
- Bridge terminals including Fleet Command, Navigation and Emergency Control;
- Docking Officers, Flight Controllers and EVA specialists;
- fire, hull damage, external repair and environmental events;
- berth control, clamps, customs and ship matching.

## G1. Controlled Port Of Entry

Required build:

- medium passenger/cargo Berth with Control and clamps;
- Gangway and Customs Counter;
- station-side Access Gate;
- Security office with Terminal;
- at least one Security Guard;
- secure route to Brig or holding room;
- ordinary public path that does not cross the inspection workspace.

Operation:

- traffic policy identifies calls requiring inspection;
- passengers/cargo queue at physical inspection slots;
- Guard presence, camera coverage and policy determine inspection throughput and
  detection quality;
- inspection may clear, confiscate, hold or reject without synthetic crime resolution;
- friction increases wait and affects public satisfaction;
- weak inspection increases theft/contraband exposure.

## G2. Emergency Service Station

Minimum readiness recipe:

- reserved Pod Dock or Berth capability;
- Clinic with Med Beds and medical staff;
- EVA staging with appropriate crew;
- fuel and parts reserve;
- redundant or protected power/air for emergency rooms;
- Bridge emergency/traffic post.

Event contract:

```text
forecast/call -> accept under policy -> reserve capacity -> arrival
  -> triage / repair / refuel / shelter -> stabilize -> release -> settlement
```

Payment includes a readiness retainer when contracted and an incident result. Idle
readiness is an intentional cost, not dead content. The player can release capacity
for commerce and accept reduced response capability.

## G3. Security And Disorder

Security operation needs:

- visible patrol posts or routes rather than global staffing counts;
- camera/data coverage linked to a staffed monitor;
- checkpoints at valuable or controlled transitions;
- evidence/confiscated-goods storage;
- Brig capacity and release/transfer policy;
- schedule coverage around arrivals, nightlife and freight peaks.

Crime pressure derives from valuable stock, crowding, dissatisfaction, district
character and weak observation. Security responds to causes and incidents; it does
not prevent all crime through an invisible aura.

## G4. Strategic Berth - Later

Required build:

- large berth and heavy clamp capacity;
- hardened Berth Control;
- secure Gangway and cargo handling;
- fuel, repair and medical support;
- Fleet Command/Navigation staffing;
- controlled storage and perimeter;
- redundant power/life support.

Possible station identities:

- neutral rescue and recovery port;
- customs/border station;
- diplomatic transfer station;
- patrol or military logistics base;
- disaster-relief staging station.

Strategic contracts may subsidize infrastructure but impose access, readiness, traffic
reservation, security or neutrality obligations. The station may always decline them.

## G5. Event Families

Initial bounded events should reuse current systems:

- damaged incoming ship;
- medical evacuation;
- debris strike or docking collision;
- solar flare and power surge;
- contraband alert;
- fire/hull breach requiring evacuation and EVA repair.

Each event has forecast, prevention, local impact, escalation and recovery. Randomness
selects among exposed systems; it does not ignore preparation.

## Strategic Choices

- commercial berth utilization versus readiness reserve;
- fast low-friction access versus thorough inspection;
- cameras and design versus labor-heavy patrols;
- accept a faction contract or preserve neutrality;
- invest in redundancy or accept lucrative operational risk;
- build own response staff or pay expensive emergency contractors.

## Failure And Recovery

- response berth occupied: divert commerce, use pod capability or fail response time;
- clinic full: triage, expand or transfer patients;
- weak checkpoint: trace incident, tighten policy or redesign route;
- power loss: emergency circuit and manual closure;
- understaffed watch: recall named crew at fatigue/morale cost;
- damaged external system: close exposed area and dispatch EVA repair;
- contract breach: penalty, relationship loss and corrective requirement.

## Acceptance Scenario

A passenger wave, freight call and emergency arrival overlap:

- the player can reserve or release response capacity through a simple policy;
- inspection occupies real staff and slots;
- emergency work uses Clinic/EVA/fuel/repair facilities physically;
- public waiting and security outcomes react to layout and staffing;
- preparedness changes event outcome without guaranteeing perfection;
- settlement reports service, delay, damage, cost and relationship honestly.

## Non-Goals

- No tactical ship combat in the first strategic slice.
- No mandatory faction campaign.
- No manual approval of every routine ship.
- No invisible `security score` resolving incidents without physical coverage.
