# Use Tenant Businesses as the First Clear Midgame Expansion

**Priority:** P1 design/UI slice; preserve existing behavior

## What worked

After the first berth payout, partitioning part of the concourse, zoning a 35-tile Commercial unit, and choosing among a bar, restaurant, and gift shop was the strongest decision in the playthrough. The proposals traded rent, revenue share, tenant staffing, capacity, and supplied goods. The chosen bar fitted itself out, brought staff, animated with customers, and measurably improved later drink service.

It connected capital, layout, demand, staffing, and revenue without requiring the player to place dozens of chairs. It also made the station feel less centrally planned.

## Missing onboarding

The system is hard to discover. Nothing in the first berth report or demand forecast suggests leasing commercial space. The relationship between a tenant's advertised capacity and actual fixture use is not yet easy to verify. The player also needs to understand what the tenant supplies versus what the station must supply.

## Required behavior

- After the first qualifying turnaround, surface a contextual opportunity to zone commercial space based on unmet demand.
- Keep zoning and proposal choice in-world; do not add a persistent spreadsheet panel.
- Proposal cards must state business type, target customers, space requirement, staffing source, goods source, rent, revenue share, and service capacity.
- Tenant fit-out must create real reservable fixtures, queues, occupancy, transactions, and visible customer behavior.
- Settlement and ledger rows must attribute tenant-provided services and income.
- Tenants should fail or leave when access, demand, utilities, or quality remain inadequate, with actionable notice.

## Acceptance criteria

- A player who sees repeated missing-drink demand can discover and open a bar without reading documentation.
- Bar, restaurant, and gift shop proposals create meaningfully different operating consequences.
- The tenant cannot receive service credit without customers using its fixtures.
- The ledger distinguishes rent from revenue share and station-operated sales.
- The commercial unit remains optional: a player-operated service path can compete at a different labor/capital tradeoff.
